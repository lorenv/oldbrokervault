How It Works
Sending: When your app sends an outgoing email, you specify a unique reply-to address (e.g., thread15@example.com) for each thread or context. You can set this using the reply_to field in the API. This unique reply-to address becomes the destination for any replies from your users.

Receiving: To handle incoming replies, you set up SendGrid’s Inbound Parse Webhook for a subdomain (e.g., parse.example.com). By configuring your domain/MX records and associating any or all addresses at that subdomain with SendGrid, you can receive emails at any address matching your pattern (like thread15@parse.example.com). SendGrid automatically parses all incoming emails to addresses under that subdomain and POSTs the contents to an endpoint you control.

Syncing/Processing: Your app can then extract the thread or context from the reply-to address (e.g., "thread15") inside the POST request, and sync the reply to the corresponding thread in your web app, providing true bidirectional sync.

Key Details
You have full flexibility on the structure of the unique reply-to address, as long as you control the subdomain and have pointed it at SendGrid’s inbound parse system.

Many SaaS and ticketing systems use this pattern (“Variable Envelope Return Path,” or VERP) to associate replies with specific conversations.

The structure is: send messages with unique reply-to addresses → receive replies at those addresses, parsed by SendGrid and POSTed to your webhook → map replies to your data model using the address.

You are not limited to static or pre-authorized addresses, as long as MX records and SendGrid settings are properly configured for wildcard or pattern-based addressing.

Limitations and Requirements
You need to own/manage the domain or subdomain and have the ability to point its MX records to SendGrid.

Unique reply-to addresses must be set up for a domain/subdomain registered with the Inbound Parse feature.

The receiving endpoint (your webhook) must handle and map the incoming emails to their relevant threads or resources.

Be careful to avoid exposing sensitive IDs or user information in reply-to addresses.
